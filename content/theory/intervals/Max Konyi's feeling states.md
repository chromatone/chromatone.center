# Max Konyi's feeling states

A granular look into Max Konyi's framework reveals that each of the 12 chromatic scale degrees acts as a distinct emotional ecosystem when measured against a steady tonic drone.

## The 12 Feeling-States in Depth

### 1. Degree 1 (The Tonic)

* **Deep Character:** Home base and the fundamental reference point around which the ear organizes all musical space. It represents the "minimum energy" state within a key—an absolute bedrock of rest and resolution where motion ceases and nothing feels left hanging or unfinished. It is the zero point of tension.

### 2. Degree 5 (The Fifth)

* **Deep Character:** The closest relative to the tonic without being the tonic itself. It is open, highly consonant, and deeply expansive, but unlike the complete stillness of home base, it pulses with a quiet, steady vitality. It anchors an entire dimension of clean, clear, and emotionally neutral sound.

### 3. Degree 3 (The Third)

* **Deep Character:** The primary pillar of major tonality. It introduces a vital axis of warmth, organic presence, and life. Rather than pure architectural neutrality, the major third radiates a soft, glowing illumination that colors the entire harmonic space around it.

### 4. Degree 2 (The Second)

* **Deep Character:** Clean, pure, and structurally sharp, yet emotionally calm and balanced. It sits cleanly in the neutral family alongside degrees 1, 4, and 5, offering a clear and defined step that feels crisp without carrying heavy emotional baggage.

### 5. Degree 4 (The Fourth)

* **Deep Character:** While it shares the clean, consonant neutrality of the fifth (being its direct inverse a fifth below the tonic), it behaves with an intentional contraction. Where the fifth feels like it opens outward into the sky, the fourth curls inward, creating a focused, inward-pulling gravity.

### 6. Degree 6 (The Sixth)

* **Deep Character:** A lighter, more softened reflection of the major third. It retains warmth and pleasantness, but in a muted, pastel, or cooler tone—acting almost like a bridge where the structural neutrality of the fourth meets the organic life of the third.

### 7. Degree 7 (The Seventh)

* **Deep Character:** Positioned just a semitone beneath the tonic, it generates a potent blend of the fifth and third. Far from just a "leading tone" demanding an immediate physical resolution to home, it possesses a standalone, bittersweet, and vibrant tension full of life and delicate longing.

### 8. Degree ♯4 / ♭5 (The Sharp Fourth / Tritone)

* **Deep Character:** A striking, high-voltage anomaly sitting right in the middle of the scale. It radiates intense, bright tension with a strange, otherworldly edge, acting as an alien, electric focal point that splits the octave right down the middle.

### 9. Degree ♭7 (The Flat Seventh)

* **Deep Character:** The lightest and most open of the flat/minor-leaning degrees. It carries a relaxed, floating tension that feels naturally bluesy, earthy, and soulful, lacking the sharp bite of heavier intervals.

### 10. Degree ♭3 (The Flat Third)

* **Deep Character:** The emotional cornerstone of minor keys. It shifts the musical landscape into a serious, solemn, and deeply introspective space, carrying a darker, more melancholic gravity than the flat seventh.

### 11. Degree ♭6 (The Flat Sixth)

* **Deep Character:** Deeper, darker, and emotionally heavier than the flat third. It introduces a profound gravity and solemn weight, pulling the listener into a somber, shadowed emotional space.

### 12. Degree ♭2 (The Flat Second)

* **Deep Character:** Melodically sitting just a single semitone above the tonic, it is intimately close by pitch yet maximally distant in emotional harmony. It creates an intensely dark, sharp, and in-your-face friction that feels provocative and arresting.
